# KisanNetra Detailed Validation Report

## 1. Scope and purpose

This report documents the model validation work completed against the project protocol in [PS-26180-Model-Validation-Protocol.md](../PS-26180-Model-Validation-Protocol.md). The work was performed inside the AI component of the repository and focuses on the disease-classification model that is used as the visual decision engine for the farm sentinel.

The purpose is not simply to report a percentage. The goal is to verify that the model’s claim is robust, internally consistent, and not dependent on a fragile evaluation path or an accidental class-order bug.

---

## 2. Model under test

- Model file: [ai/best_model.pth](best_model.pth)
- Classes: Bacterial Blight, Blast, Brown Spot, Healthy, Tungro
- Device: CUDA-enabled GPU (`cuda:0`)
- Architecture: MobileNetV3-Small

The model is the trained disease classifier used by the AI core. The validation is designed to test the model as a real decision engine, not just as a lab artifact.

---

## 3. Source of evidence

The validation output is stored in [ai/validation_report.json](validation_report.json), generated by [ai/model_validation_suite.py](model_validation_suite.py).

The report contains measurements for:

- statistical confidence
- calibration quality
- confusion analysis
- independent reproduction
- perturbation robustness
- rule-engine sanity checks
- regression lock against class-order drift

This is a real validation bundle rather than a single accuracy number.

---

## 4. Statistical confidence results

### 4.1 Overall test accuracy

The validation suite computed an overall accuracy of 87.70% on the held-out test set.

### 4.2 Confidence interval

The 95% Wilson confidence interval was:

- lower bound: 0.8552
- upper bound: 0.8959

This means the true test-set accuracy likely lies in the range of approximately 85.5% to 89.6%, not exactly 87.7%.

### 4.3 Bootstrap result

The bootstrap estimate produced:

- mean accuracy: 87.68%
- 95% bootstrap interval: 85.60% to 89.65%

This is important because it gives an empirical estimate of uncertainty instead of relying on a single test split.

### Interpretation

The model is clearly better than chance and performs strongly on the held-out data. However, the point estimate is not treated as a guaranteed truth; it sits inside a measured uncertainty band. That is the correct engineering posture for a model that is being used in a field decision-support system.

---

## 5. Calibration analysis

The Expected Calibration Error (ECE) was measured at 0.0369.

This is a fairly good calibration result for a small multiclass classification system. It indicates that the model’s confidence levels are not wildly divorced from actual correctness; the confidence scores are reasonably meaningful.

### Why calibration matters here

The project includes a differential fusion layer that uses confidence as part of the reasoning process. If a model outputs high confidence that is not actually reliable, the fusion engine may trust the wrong signal. The measured ECE suggests that this risk is not severe in the current model, although a full reliability diagram would still be a good future enhancement.

## 7. Confusion matrix and class-level interpretation

The confusion matrix in the report shows the distribution of errors across the five classes.

### Key pattern

The most common confusion pairs were:

1. Brown Spot → Blast: 22
2. Blast → Tungro: 19
3. Brown Spot → Bacterial Blight: 12
4. Brown Spot → Tungro: 11
5. Bacterial Blight → Tungro: 9
6. Tungro → Blast: 9
7. Blast → Bacterial Blight: 8

### Meaning

These are not random, unstructured mistakes. They are systematic confusions between classes that can look visually similar under field conditions.

This is agronomically meaningful because plant diseases often share lesion structure, color variation, and environmental stress patterns. A model that confuses related diseases is not “bad”; it is revealing a real limitation of the visual signal.

The macro F1 score was 0.8627, which supports the conclusion that the model is broadly strong but still has understandable confusion patterns in certain disease pairs.

## 8. Perturbation robustness

> **CORRECTED.** The figures previously published in this section were produced
> by a harness bug and the conclusion drawn from them was wrong. Both are
> retained below so the correction is auditable.

### The bug

`evaluate_perturbations()` applied each perturbation to an **already normalized**
tensor. `torchvision.adjust_brightness` and `adjust_contrast` blend against a
constant and then **clamp the result to [0, 1]**, but a normalized tensor spans
roughly [-2.1, +2.6]. Measured on this test set, clamping pinned **63.9%** of
pixels (brightness) and **80.8%** of pixels (contrast) to a clamp boundary,
destroying the image before it reached the model. `gaussian_blur` and `rotate`
do not clamp, which is precisely why only those two ever looked sane.

The fix applies perturbations to the [0, 1] image and normalizes afterwards.

### Results

Sample raised from 64 to 400 images, with a clean baseline measured on the
same sample so each drop is like-for-like, and 95% Wilson intervals throughout.

| Perturbation | Previously published (buggy, n=64) | Corrected (n=400) | 95% Wilson CI | Drop vs clean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *clean baseline* | not measured | 86.75% | [83.08%, 89.73%] | — |
| blur | 68.75% | 69.00% | [64.30%, 73.33%] | **-17.75 pp** |
| low brightness | 26.56% | 85.25% | [81.44%, 88.39%] | -1.50 pp |
| high contrast | 20.31% | 86.75% | [83.08%, 89.73%] | 0.00 pp |
| rotation (10°) | 81.25% | 86.75% | [83.08%, 89.73%] | 0.00 pp |

### Interpretation

The previous interpretation — "the model degrades substantially under brightness
and contrast distortion" and "is sensitive to lighting conditions" — **is
withdrawn.** It described a clamping artefact, not the model. Under the mild
shifts actually tested (brightness x0.7, contrast x1.4) the model is close to
unaffected.

**Blur is the real weakness, and it was hidden behind the two spurious
collapses.** A -17.75 pp drop under a 5x5 Gaussian blur is the only material
degradation in this set, and its interval [64.30%, 73.33%] does not come close
to overlapping the clean baseline. That points at focus and image sharpness,
not illumination:

- fixed-focus optics set for a known canopy standoff distance
- a rigid mount, so the frame is not softened by movement during capture
  (the node is a fixed sentinel, which helps here by construction)
- a sharpness gate that rejects and re-captures a soft frame rather than
  classifying it

### What this does and does not say about the sun hood

The old text of this section recommended "lens shielding or sun hood" as a
deployment mitigation. That recommendation no longer follows from anything
measured here, and is withdrawn.

The sun hood itself is unaffected, because it never depended on this section.
It is argued from domain shift in
[`docs/00_initial_technical_assessment.md`](../docs/00_initial_technical_assessment.md) §4
and [`docs/22_power_autonomy.md`](../docs/22_power_autonomy.md), and those
arguments stand on their own. The point is only that **this test must not be
cited as evidence for it** — the perturbation range tested (x0.7 brightness)
is far milder than real overexposure, so these numbers say nothing either way
about harsh sunlight.

This is exactly the kind of result that matters when the project is framed as a field-ready system rather than a clean lab demo.

## 9. Rule-engine sanity checks

The rule engine was also tested against key scenarios. The suite verified:

1. dry soil leads to irrigation action
2. rain blocks irrigation
3. nutrient shortage produces alerts
4. disease fusion distinguishes HIGH-risk disease and healthy state

All checks passed.

### Why this matters

The AI model and the sensor logic are not being treated as disconnected components. The validation suite ensures that the disease output and the rule system can work together in defensible scenarios.

This matters because a vision model may be strong in isolation while the downstream intervention logic fails or invents sensor values. The validation report confirms that the rule engine behaves correctly in the tested scenarios.

---

## 10. Regression lock

The regression lock check also passed.

That check ensures:

- the class ordering in `classes.json` remains consistent with the model output dimension
- the class list is not silently drifting again

This is important because the project has already experienced a class-order bug in the past. The regression check acts as a safeguard to prevent silent reintroduction.

---

## 11. Overall engineering assessment

### What is good

The current model appears to be a legitimate working prototype. The key strengths are:

- strong held-out accuracy near 87.7%
- good macro F1 value
- independent reproduction passed
- confusion matrix indicates reasonable, structured failure modes
- class-order regression is protected
- rule-engine logic behaves correctly in tested scenarios

### What remains incomplete

The protocol also requires true out-of-distribution validation with new unseen field images. That step was not performed in this session because no fresh unseen photo set was collected.

This means the model is not yet fully “field-proven,” even though it is clearly stronger than a random or broken baseline. In a final technical story, the honest phrasing should be:

> The model is validated on a held-out split, internally reproduced, statistically stable, and reasonably calibrated, but full unseen-field validation remains the next required step.

That is the accurate engineering position.

---

## 12. Decision summary

### Current verdict

The model is in a strong prototype state and passes the validation protocol’s available checks.

### Most important evidence

- accuracy: 87.70%
- 95% CI: 85.5% to 89.6%
- bootstrap CI: 85.6% to 89.7%
- independent reproduction: pass
- ECE: 0.0369
- regression lock: pass
- rule-engine checks: pass

### Remaining requirement to complete the protocol fully

The only gap that remains in the strict protocol is the acquisition of a truly unseen field-image set for an OOD holdout test.

Until that is done, the project can fairly claim supported internal validation, but not full external field validation.

---

## 13. Final statement

The AI model is no longer a weak or suspicious prototype. It has survived the core validation checks required by the protocol and shows a stable, explainable, internally consistent classification performance. The main remaining step is to validate it on genuinely unseen field images so that the final claim becomes fully field-ready rather than only well validated on the available split.
